why the numbers move like that

Current is linear in duty and the eye is a power law, so the host drives a value meant to read as half as bright at 0.52.2, about 22 per cent. Half brightness is therefore roughly a fifth of the current, which is why a supply sized for the honest worst case is nowhere near the datasheet's.

18 A is that datasheet worst case, 13 A is what real WS2815 draws at full white, and a show sits far under both: the mixer holds a house floor and compresses its own highlights, so all 720 pixels are never white at once. Idle with every LED dark is about 1 W for the whole frame.

White is also the worst case by colour, because it lights all three dies. A saturated cue lights one.

why three data lines, and the split

One line is 30 us per LED, so all 720 on one is 21.6 ms and the room caps near 46 fps. Splitting three ways puts the longest write at 9.0 ms of a 16.7 ms frame.

A and B start on diagonally opposite corners because the perimeter is walked continuously and this cuts it in half. No box position makes both short, so both get a buffered twisted pair and neither is a daisy chain. The beam gets its own line for the same reason: fed off the end of Frame W it would need three and a half metres of dark jumper routed back around the frame to reach its own first pixel.

The committed firmware splits 300/420 across two lines. Going to three is a change to LINE_A/LINE_B in fixture/frame.rs, one more PIO state machine and one more DMA channel.

why the lengths come out exact

WS2815 at 60/m cuts every 16.67 mm, and 180 x 16.67 = 3000.0 mm exactly. No fractional cuts anywhere on the fixture.

A 5 m reel is 300 LEDs, which is exactly one long side plus one short side. Reel 1 gives N and E, reel 2 gives S and W, reel 3 gives the beam and 3 m of spare. Order every reel in one purchase: WS2815 batches differ visibly in white, and a bin step at a corner is the sort of thing you cannot unsee.

Hanging it

  • Wire rope with a turnbuckle at each frame end, not threaded rod.
  • Anchor into rafters or collar ties, never into battens or sheathing.
  • Splay the wires outward. Four corners plus the midpoint of each 3 m side.
why not rod, and why splayed

A pitched roof gives every anchor a different height, so rod means six lengths each cut once, and any error tilts a 3 x 2 m object at eye level. A turnbuckle levels it in five minutes.

Vertical wires let 12 kg swing like a pendulum every time a door shuts. Splaying triangulates it, and it also means the anchor does not have to be directly above the point it holds - which is exactly the freedom a slope needs.

If nothing lands where you need it, screw a batten between two rafters and hang from that. About 300 Kc, and it turns an alignment problem into a free choice.

Wiring

  • One SN74AHCT125 for all three lines, 100 ohm in series on each output.
  • Each line on its own twisted pair in CAT5e, data and its own ground.
  • Tie the arriving data to both DI and BI at every run's input.
  • Ten injection points, both ends of every run. Five fuses, one per run.

The level shifter is not optional

WS2815 wants 3.5 V of logic high and the Pico gives 3.3. Without a shifter the strip usually works, which is worse than failing: it fails later, intermittently, and looks like a network fault.

why both ends, both data pins, and a fuse each

Both ends. A 3 m run at full white pulls 3.2 A, and end-fed through the strip's own copper that is a 2 to 3 V drop by the far end. Fed from both, under 1 V.

Both data pins. WS2815's backup line is why one dead LED does not take out the rest of the run, and the first pixel only gets that protection if you wire DI and BI together at the input.

A fuse each. A 17 A supply behind thin strip copper will happily set fire to a short. Five 5 A blade fuses at the box is 350 Kc and it is the difference between a fault and an incident.

Its own ground per pair. Three data wires sharing one return is the classic intermittent-glitch build.

Building it

  • Each 3 m side is two 1.5 m halves, spliced where the beam attaches.
  • Buy 2 m profile lengths, never 3 m.
  • Opal cover, not clear.

The aluminium profile is a heatsink, not trim

WS2815 dissipates 13 to 18 W per metre at full white and wood is an insulator. The wall switch makes this a room light: pick a pale colour, leave it four hours, and that is sustained full load - the one thing a music-reactive fixture normally never does.

why plywood on edge, why the splice lands there, why opal

Plywood, not a spruce batten. Calculated deflection of an 80 x 12 mm rib under its own weight plus the profile is about 7 mm over an unsupported 3 m span, and under a millimetre with a hanger at the midpoint. Timber's problem is not sag but twist: a bow drops the light line a few millimetres and nobody notices, a twist aims it sideways and everybody does.

The splice. Czech couriers cap at 2 m, so a 3 m rib has to be two pieces anyway. Splitting at the midpoint puts the joint exactly where the beam's bracket goes, so it hides behind hardware you were fitting regardless.

Opal. At 2.4 m a standing person's eyes are 60 cm below the strip and looking straight at it. Bare WS2815 at 60/m from there is 720 point sources at eye level. The cover costs about 30 per cent of the output and is worth it.

Check two things before ordering profile: that the internal channel is wider than your strip's PCB (WS2815 comes as 10 mm or 12 mm), and how deep it is. Deeper blends the dots into a continuous line; shallower keeps travelling-pixel effects sharper. Only the glare is non-negotiable.

Still open

  • Which way the ridge runs relative to the 3 m axis. Do not cut ribs until this is known.
  • Roof height above the frame, and whether there are collar ties.
  • The white point. WS2815 has no white emitter, so equal duty is not white.
what to do about each

The ridge. If the 2 m axis sits across the slope there is clearance to spare. If the 3 m axis does, the corners may foul the roof, and the fix is either rotating the frame or moving it off centre - both of which change where the ribs get cut.

The height. Collar ties are the prize: horizontal, at a sane height, and a short vertical drop off them levels itself. Rafters work but need splayed wire.

The white point. Set the resting colour to zero saturation, put it beside something you agree is warm white, and cut green and blue until it stops arguing. Cut those two rather than red: correcting a cool cast by pulling red down spends the channel the eye has least of and leaves the fixture dimmer for it.
